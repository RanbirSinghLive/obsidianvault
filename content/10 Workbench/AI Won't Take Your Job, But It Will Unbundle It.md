I hear a lot of fear about AI from folks in my circles. The themes is being wholesale replaced by AI. I think the root of this anxiety comes from two places. Not understanding how current AI works and what it's trend forecasted to do in pragmatic forecasts. The second is looking at yourself as a two part bundle you plus your current job. The first fear can't be assuaged in a short essay but the second definitely can.

AI refactors code by breaking down components into parts and deleting, adding, and reconstituting the new components. Since [[Everything Is A Codebase]], it will look at you in the same way. And it'll be wise to thing so.

Your job is actually a bundle of tasks for a while you possess a bundle of skills. AI will break each down and reconstitute new wholes.

Until the 1970's large firms had dedicated pools of typists, sometimes numbering in the hundreds. Executives would handwrite deals and send them to the typists, routed based in priority, specialization, and skill level. The typists would transcribe the deal on carbon paper and send it back.

When personal computers with word processors arrived, typists got unbundled in different ways. The executives simply became typists on the side, eliminated the lag between ideation and execution. The former typists became administrative assistants, advanced formatters, paralegals and technical writers. Those who thrived took their typing skill and layered on domain knowledge. Tech unbundled the exec and typist jobs. It took the typing function and incorporated it into the exec function. It left alone the rest of people's complexity or it left nothing for those who had none.

When you eventually come around and lay out your bundle of tasks and examine them, you'll want to know what's AI-proof and AI-kindling. Here's a useful heuristic: are you romancing data or romancing people?

Those who romance data are living in spreadsheets, doing things like analyzing, optimizing, forecasting. AI has much more rizz with numbers than you.

Those who romance people are living on Teams calls, doing things like negotiating, mediating, selling. AI will take a lot longer to steal their swagger.

Humans will still be at the top of the hierarchy (some humans). They will be the ones to direct capital after AI gives them the game-theory-optimal move, and they might not listen. Humans have biases, insecurities and principles. They will still interface with humans, but only the ones who know how to romance them.

So now you're evaluated your skills and rated them on how they fall in this spectrum. You may think you've just chucked away half your worth, but you'd be wrong. Having one AI-resilient skill you are a market leader at is enough. Geoff Hinton, father of modern AI, famously advised no one train to be a radiologoist in YEAR, due to AI being way better at analyzing them. Now X years later, there are more radiologists than ever getting paid more than ever. This bad prophecy fell victim assuming there's a fixed pie of demand for radiology scans (SP). It turns out we were bottlenecked and want to do exponentially more scans than previous tech allowed. Each scan still requires a small amount of human work for patient interaction, review and edge cases. Now radiologists just process way more scans than previously and are stickier than ever. Think about how much more of your top skill you could do if you were unburdened.

The next checksum on your talent bundle should be understanding the scarcity of your bundle. You may not get confident understanding if you're world class at one skill, but here's a reframe. Look for the intersections of skills you are both pretty good at. For example Scott Adams said he was an ok cartonnist and office culturist. Combining both led to Dilbert which broke out as a top comic. Look for a scarce overlap of your AI-resilient skills.

And I must insist, don't underestimate AI. Don't assume the past performance of ai is what it can do today or what it'll do in the future. It's getting better every day. Don't run from AI, run towards it. There will be places you can run to. The overhang of places not disrupted will be huge and surprisingly resilient, but the overhang will collapse into an avalanche in your lifetime. 

Start using AI to make efficiencies in your own personal life first. Unbundle your tasks and skills and find the use case that'll save you 5 minutes per week. Then brag about your wins online. This will be the first rep you need to start thinking like your employer wants it's AI zealots to think. The work disruption won't come from your AI team. It'll come from people with the domain knowledge and courage to disrupt. Just don't be quiet about it. You have the once-in-a-lifetime chance to make the playbook for how AI will disrupt your job, but don't do it haphazardly. Unbundle your job and your skillset to match yourself to a structural moat, then do all in on accelerating it.
