Alejandra’s car sped past an abandoned police station, its emblem being slowly scrubbed away by Nevada sand.

There used to be people here. The metal, tail-fin shaped sign on the empty diner hinted that the town had been built for atoms. The nuclear tests of the 50s brought people here to serve the needs of military men. Then the atoms shifted from predator to provider and a cutting edge nuclear plant was built. The town metastasized around it. An elementary school tool kids until they were old enough to bus to the high school in the larger town over. Houses and shops and utilities.

Then one day they all had to leave. The plant was no longer theres. Its not that it melted down. It was needed for something else. Something not human.[^1]

The road snaking the edge of town and slingshotting off its orbits towards the plant was the most visible. Traffic hasnt slowed here, but instead grown. It grew like all things about this unhuman thing grew - vertical and fast. Alejandra figured it she had to run there was plenty of temporary cover in the town. The car was a Gravitas Corral, driverless with the two front seats pointed inwards to create a mobile lounge. The steering wheel and navigation all stripped away so it felt like humans had never been allowed to pilot these things. The blue dot on Alejandra’s progress tracker was on top of her destination, the map not having been updated to see it yet.

“Maam, the pressure is mounting to ease the export ban,” Jeremy said

“This again,” Alejandra said.

“I hate to he repetitive but they need an answer beyond national security,. We have South American drought modelling and Vietnamese neonatal scanning projects begging for compute. We have to show AI is for non-white countries too. These are real lives we could save,” Jeremy said.

“We’re UNESCO, I think the term you’re looking for is international security,” Alejandra said.

The twin gray cooling towers of the plant billowed furious steam towards the heavens. The containment structure had its lights on to ward off infiltrators. The turbine was wired up to a transformer with a long stretch of transmission towers spanning oit onto the horizon, towards civilization. However, Alejandra knew they were mirages and carried no signal. Somewhere in the coiled wiring nest would be a newer transmission vector, towards the newer sruc that barely poked out above the mesa on unzoned land beside the plant. The security gate in front of the structure was bigger than itself.

The car stops in front of the gate and doesnt move. Alejandra presses the support button out of sight under her armrest, designed to not be found unless you look really hard for it.

‘Thanks for contacting Gravitas support-‘ the car said.

“Put me through to a human,” Alejandra said.

‘No humans work in this department but-‘

“Im in front of my destination but the car isnt moving in”

The cartoony sound that bots makes when thinking happens.

‘I see you are in an abandoned town with the nuclear plant still running. Are you here to visit the plant or are you lost?’

“Neither,” Alejandra said, offering no further help.

The car pulled up and they each had to show ID. Alejandra pulled out her TCAG badge and got a retinal scan from the guard. He was military instead of corporate, no doubt a new measure. She looked at her badge picture which showed her long black hair well. They’d let you smile in you pic or be goofy, a sign of the Bay culture but there was no bat in sight here.

The structure was a dug out that sloped down into the ground with newly paved rode. They drove into the belly slowly. At the dark end of the slope two blast doors slid open to reveal a parkade. There were a dozen delivery trucks in various states of unload, inspection and departure prep on the left side. The ride side housed a few cars for visiting execs but the majority of people weren’t supposed to leave.

Security took what TSA did at the airport and did it right. Full strip search and scanning but all robotic so no person had to pat the inside of your bra.

The false crowns on two of her molars had held in place. The only contraband she needed waited underneath, a micro-transmitter waiting to be assembled.
